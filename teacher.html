PAPER TRAIL · TEACHER GUIDE

A 20-minute immigration card game

Students play a fictional immigrant on their own Chromebook, side by side with a partner. They open card packs, collect forms and documents, and file them in the right order. Along the way they learn how U.S. immigration pathways actually work, and why two people doing the same things can have very different outcomes.

▶ Open the game

Learning goals

Students will be able to:

  1. Sequence the main steps of two real pathways: an employment-based green card (H-1B → PERM → I-140 → Visa Bulletin → I-485 → citizenship) and the H-2A seasonal guest worker program.
  2. Identify which agency handles each step (USCIS, Department of Labor, State Department, CBP) and who files it (the worker or the employer).
  3. Explain how per-country caps, lotteries, fees, processing backlogs and job ties affect how long immigration takes.
  4. Evaluate an immigration policy using evidence from their own play: who benefits, who waits, and what they would change.

Connects to the C3 Framework: D2.Civ.13.6-8 (purposes, implementation and consequences of public policies), D2.Civ.12.6-8 (laws and rights), D2.Geo.8.6-8 (human migration), D2.Eco.1.6-8 (costs and benefits of decisions).

Run of show (about 20 minutes)

0:00
Set up (2 min). Put the table code on the board (for example, MAPLE). Students pair up, open the game, type their first name and the code, and pick a character. Assign pairings (below) so partners compare different characters.
0:02
Year 1 together (3 min). Everyone reads the intro, opens their first pack, and files their first form. Yellow tip bubbles guide the first year.
0:05
Play (10 min). 12 years, about 45–60 seconds each. At each year's end, the game shows a "Partner check" screen. Partners wait for each other, compare, then start the next year together.
0:15
Debrief (5 min). Collect end-screen results on a class chart (each student reads out their one-line summary). Use the discussion questions below.

Short on time? Stop after Year 8. The Priya/Lukas gap is already clear by then.

Table codes (scenarios)

Partners must type the same code. The code sets the "world news" for each year, and for Priya and Lukas it also sets the lottery results. That way both computers run the same scenario without any internet connection between them. Card packs are still random for each player. You can also share a link with the code built in, for example index.html?code=CEDAR.

Characters & recommended pairings

🇮🇳 Priya · IndiaSoftware engineer with a U.S. master's degree. H-1B lottery → employer-sponsored green card (EB-2). She usually gets an approved I-140, then waits in the India EB-2 line, which is decades long. Rare cards let her try the EB-1 category or move to Canada.
🇩🇪 Lukas · GermanySame degree, same employer, same forms as Priya. Germany's EB-2 line was "Current" in 2026, so he usually gets a green card and may reach citizenship within the 12 years.
🇲🇽 Marco · MexicoH-2A seasonal farmworker. Each year he needs a visa and must cross at a port of entry to work the season, then return home. He saves toward a family house. H-2A does not lead to a green card; a rare year-round sponsor card can open the EB-3 path.

Pairings

  • Priya vs. Lukas: "Same job, different country." The clearest lesson: identical choices, very different results because of the 7% per-country cap.
  • Marco vs. Lukas: "Two kinds of worker visa." A seasonal visa with no path vs. a skilled-worker visa with a path.
  • Marco vs. Priya: "Two long roads." Both work in the U.S. for years; both hit walls they can't control.

How the game teaches

🔍 Hidden stepsThe Paper Trail shows each next step only as "? ? ?" plus clues: the agency and who files it. Students read the cards' "When to use it" text to work out the order.
❌ Rejections cost moneyFiling too early gets rejected. The fee is lost (as in real life) and a Rejection Notice clogs the hand.
📨 Requests for EvidenceThe right form without the right documents triggers an RFE. Students must find the document before the deadline.
⏳ Wait cards"Date Not Current" cards can't be played or discarded. Priya's hand fills with them while she waits in line, so the backlog takes up her hand space.
🎁 ChoicesEach year students draft a card: lawyers, legal clinics, premium processing, savings, or rare "legendary" choices such as moving to Canada.
🚩 Scam awarenessA "Fast Visa Help!" notario card promises guaranteed approval. Students who read carefully spot the red flags.

Debrief questions

  1. What decided how far you got: your choices, luck, money, or where you were born? Give one example from your game.
  2. Priya and Lukas filed the same forms for the same employer. Why did one usually get a green card and the other didn't? Is the 7% per-country limit fair?
  3. Marco worked in the U.S. for many seasons but could not get a green card. Should years of seasonal work count toward permanent residency? What would farms, workers and taxpayers say?
  4. Which step surprised you the most? Why do you think the law works that way?
  5. Employers file many of these steps. What power does that give employers over workers? Is that good, bad, or both?
  6. If you were in Congress, what ONE rule would you change? Who would support it, and who would object?

Class chart

Every end screen shows a one-line summary like PAPER TRAIL · Table MAPLE · Priya · Waiting in line (3/5) · 12 yrs · $76,000 · 1 rejections. Make a chart on the board with columns for character, security level and money, and have students add their result. The pattern by character is often the strongest evidence for the discussion.

Key vocabulary

VisaPermission to travel to the U.S. and ask to enter for a specific purpose.
Green cardLawful permanent residency. You can live and work in the U.S. permanently.
H-1BTemporary work visa for jobs that need a college degree. 85,000 new visas a year, given by lottery.
H-2ATemporary visa for seasonal farm work. No yearly limit; the worker is tied to one employer.
PERMLabor certification: the employer proves no qualified U.S. worker is available.
Priority dateYour place in the green card line.
Visa BulletinA monthly State Department chart showing which priority dates can get green cards now.
Per-country capNo country can get more than 7% of green cards in a year.
RFERequest for Evidence: the government asks for missing documents.
NaturalizationBecoming a U.S. citizen after holding a green card, usually for 5 years.
Notario fraudA scam where someone who is not a lawyer offers immigration "help."

Classroom sensitivity

  • Some students may be immigrants or have family members with different immigration statuses. Never ask students about their own or their family's status, and let them opt out of sharing personal connections.
  • The characters are fictional composites. The game does not simulate raids, detention or deportation. When a character "has to leave," it's shown as the end of a visa, not as enforcement.
  • This game covers two legal pathways only: employment-based and seasonal worker. It does not cover family sponsorship, refugees, asylum, the diversity lottery or unauthorized immigration. Mention that these exist and work differently.
  • Frame the debrief around systems and policies, not around whether people "deserve" to immigrate.

Accuracy & simplifications

Facts, fees and waiting lines are based on official sources as of September 2026: the USCIS fee schedule (Form G-1055), the September 2026 Visa Bulletin, DOL processing data, and Federal Register rules. Immigration policy changes often, and several 2026 rules were in court or only proposed when this was written.

Things the game simplifies

  • Time moves in 1-year turns, so processing times are rounded (for example, PERM ≈ 2 years, I-140 and I-485 ≈ 1 year).
  • Money is shown as savings after rent, taxes and living costs, not gross salary. Fees are approximate (paper-filing amounts).
  • The Visa Bulletin moves at a steady, simplified speed. Real movement is irregular and can go backward.
  • The H-1B lottery results are scripted in the named scenarios so that partner comparisons are fair. Real odds were about 35% in the 2025 lottery.
  • Family members (spouses and children, who also use up green card numbers) are left out.
  • Priya's "Move to Canada" and "EB-1" cards stand for real choices many backlogged workers make, in a very simplified form.

Key facts used

  • 85,000 new H-1B visas a year (65,000 + 20,000 for U.S. advanced degrees); about 344,000 registrations and a ~35% selection rate in the FY2026 lottery; wage-weighted selection since Feb 2026. USCIS · Federal Register
  • Fees: H-1B registration $215; I-129 H-1B about $3,380 with required employer fees; I-140 $715 + $600; I-485 $1,440; N-400 $760; premium processing $2,965 (since Mar 2026). USCIS G-1055
  • Sept 2026 Visa Bulletin: EB-2 rest of world "Current"; EB-2 India unavailable (last cutoff July 15, 2014); EB-3 Other Workers Mexico Apr 1, 2022. State Dept.
  • 7% per-country cap (25,620); at least 140,000 employment-based green cards a year including family members. A 2026 NFAP projection put the potential EB-2 wait for Indians filing in 2026 at 179 years. NFAP
  • H-2A: no cap; a record 398,258 positions certified in FY2025; about 90% of visas go to Mexican workers; employers must provide housing and transportation and reimburse visa and travel costs; 3-year maximum stay, then 60 days outside the U.S. DOL · USCIS
  • The 2025 AEWR (H-2A minimum wage) rule lowered wages in most states; a federal court ruled it unlawful in Aug 2026 but left rates in place for now.
  • $100,000 H-1B proclamation (Sept 2025): students changing status inside the U.S. were exempt; vacated by a federal court in June 2026; a separate $103,265 fee was proposed in Aug 2026.
  • 2025 civics test: up to 20 questions from 128; 12 correct to pass. USCIS
  • Notario fraud: "A notario público is not authorized to provide you with any legal services related to immigration." USCIS
