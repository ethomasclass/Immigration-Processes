Paper Trail
Teacher Guide

A 20-minute card game. Students play a made-up immigrant on their own Chromebook, next to a partner. They open packs, put forms in order, and make one hard choice each turn. Students leave knowing the process is hard to navigate, without having to memorize every form.

Open the game

Learning goals

  1. Describe the main steps of two legal paths: a skilled worker visa that can lead to a green card (H-1B), and a seasonal farm worker visa (H-2A) that does not.
  2. Explain why the process is hard: lotteries, missing papers, rejections, fees, employer control and long waiting lines.
  3. Compare how country of birth changes the wait (the 7% per-country limit).
  4. Evaluate one immigration rule using evidence from the game.

Connects to the C3 Framework: D2.Civ.13.6-8 (public policy), D2.Geo.8.6-8 (migration), D2.Eco.1.6-8 (costs and benefits of choices).

Run of show · 20 minutes

0:00
Set up (2 min). Write a table code on the board, like MAPLE. Partners sit together, type the same code, and pick different players.
0:02
Turn 1 together (3 min). Yellow tip bubbles walk students through opening a pack and playing the first card.
0:05
Play turns 2–8 (10 min). About 1.5 minutes per turn. Each turn: read the phone, open a pack, play cards, make one choice, check in with your partner.
0:15
Talk (5 min). Students read their one-line result aloud for a class chart. Use the questions below.

How a turn works

📱 PhoneEach turn starts with news, a text from family, and updates from the government ("Case approved!").
🃏 PackStudents tear open a pack of 4 cards: forms, papers, money, bills, help, and "stuck" wait cards.
🛣️ RoadStudents drag forms onto their road in order. The glowing space shows the next step. Later steps stay hidden, and playing one too early gets it rejected and loses the fee.
🔔 Now ServingOnce a player gets a ticket number, a deli-style sign shows how far the green card line has moved. Priya's sign still shows 2014.
⚖️ DecisionOne real dilemma per turn: fly home to see a sick grandparent? Report missing pay? Pay a "notario"?
🤝 Partner checkAt the end of each turn, partners compare status and money before moving on.
⭐ Top TalentSkilled workers can collect Top Talent cards (patents, awards, news stories). With 3, they can file a Top Talent Petition themselves. It's based on the real EB-1 "extraordinary ability" green card, which needs proof in at least 3 of 10 areas. For India, it's one of the few shortcuts out of a decades-long line.
🪜 Status ladderThe score is how high you climb: Temporary → Work visa → In line → Green card. Getting a ticket number counts. Most Priyas end at level 3, and that is the lesson, not a failure.
⏩ Fast forwardIf a player is still in line when the game ends, the sign fast-forwards until their number is called. For example: "Priya gets a green card in 2074. She is 72 years old." Marco's fast-forward shows him still working seasons years later.

Pairings

Priya + LukasSame degree, same job, same forms. Lukas's line (Germany) had no wait in 2026. Priya's (India) was stuck at 2014.

Marco + PriyaBoth work in the U.S. for years. Both hit walls they can't control.

Discussion questions

  1. What decided how far you got: your choices, luck, money, or where you were born?
  2. How old was your character in the fast-forward? How would waiting that long change someone's life?
  3. Priya and Lukas did the same things. Is it fair that every country gets the same number of green cards?
  4. Marco worked many seasons but could never stay. Should years of seasonal work count toward a green card?
  5. Employers file most of these steps. What power does that give them over workers?
  6. Which choice was hardest for you? Why?
  7. If you could change one rule, what would it be? Who would agree and who would disagree?

Classroom care

  • Some students may be immigrants or have family members with different statuses. Never ask students about their own or their family's status.
  • The characters are made up. The game has no raids, detention or deportation. When a character leaves, it is because a visa ended.
  • The game covers two legal work paths only. Family sponsorship, refugees, asylum and the diversity lottery work differently. Mention that they exist.
  • Keep the talk on rules and systems, not on whether people "deserve" to immigrate.

Accuracy

Facts, fees and wait times come from official sources as of September 2026. Rules change often, and several 2026 rules were in court or only proposed.

Simplified for the game

  • 8 turns of about 1.5 years each. Processing times are rounded to whole turns.
  • Money is savings after rent, taxes and food. Fees are approximate paper-filing amounts.
  • The "Now Serving" line moves at a steady speed. Real lines move unevenly and sometimes backward.
  • Lottery results are scripted in the named table codes so partner comparisons are fair. Real odds were about 35% in 2025.
  • Top Talent is simplified: in real life, EB-1 proof is judged case by case, and most applicants hire lawyers. The fast-forward uses the game's steady line speed, not an official forecast.
  • Spouses and children, who also use up green cards, are left out. H-1B extensions and document expiration dates are left out too.

Key facts

  • 85,000 new H-1B visas a year. About 344,000 entries in the 2025 lottery. Since Feb 2026, higher wage levels get more entries. USCIS
  • Fees: lottery $215; I-129 about $3,380 with employer fees; I-140 $715 + $600; I-485 $1,440; premium processing $2,965. USCIS G-1055
  • September 2026 Visa Bulletin: EB-2 for most countries "Current"; EB-2 India stuck at July 15, 2014; EB-3 Other Workers (Mexico) April 1, 2022. State Dept.
  • 7% per-country cap (25,620). A 2026 NFAP projection put the EB-2 wait for an Indian filing in 2026 at 179 years (a projection, not a prediction). NFAP
  • H-2A: no cap; a record 398,258 jobs certified in FY2025; about 90% of visas go to Mexican workers; employers must provide housing and travel and may not charge recruiting fees. DOL
  • EB-3 "Other Workers" (year-round jobs only) is capped at 10,000 a year worldwide.
  • 1% federal tax on cash remittances since January 1, 2026. Bank transfers are exempt.
  • Notario fraud: "A notario público is not authorized to provide you with any legal services related to immigration." USCIS

Tech notes
